## Part 6 — Audio Direction (music, ambience, effects, mixing)

### 6.1 Principles
1. **Every sound is generated by our code.** Music is performed live from composed material. Instruments and effects are synthesized by the **Sound Foundry**. No samples are recorded or downloaded.
2. **Snow is quiet.** The mix breathes: soft transients, air, space, and silence. Loud things (the tree falling) are rare and earned.
3. **Nothing repeats identically.** Every one-shot has 4–10 rendered variants plus runtime pitch, gain and filter randomization. Music is generative, and there is intentional silence.
4. **Diegetic music-making.** Stacking, clean splits, the wind chime, the radio, Elin's window, humming NPCs and the hall piano all play *in the current key*. The world and the score share one harmonic space.
5. **Audio and visuals share signals.** One wind gust signal drives both. Animation events trigger sounds, never timers.

### 6.2 Audio engine architecture (`src/view/audio/`; pure parts in `src/music/` and `src/dsp/`)
- **AudioContext** at 48 kHz, `latencyHint: "interactive"`. It is unlocked by the boot splash click or keypress.
- **Graph:**
```
[sources] → category buses → Master (gentle glue comp, then limiter) → destination

Buses:
  Music     (own reverb send to MusicHall IR; duck gain; interior EQ)
  Ambience  (outdoor: occlusion LPF; indoor sub-bus)
  SFX-World (positional; sends to the zone reverb: Valley / Forest / Room / Hall / Sauna)
  Voice     (NPC murmur blips; dry + small room send)
  UI        (dry, non-positional)
  Radio     (diegetic; band-pass, saturation, wow/flutter; routed as SFX when positional)
```
- **Reverbs:** ConvolverNodes with **procedurally generated impulse responses**. Each IR is built from exponentially decaying stereo-decorrelated noise with band-dependent decay, plus an early-reflection pattern.

| IR | Decay | Character | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Valley | 2.8 s | Sparse; **discrete echoes at 380 ms and 610 ms**, low-passed (the ridge answers your axe) | Outdoors, open |
| Forest | 1.2 s | Damped highs | Woodlot, ridge forest |
| Room-small | 0.5 s | — | Cabin |
| Room-wood | 0.9 s | — | Shops |
| Hall | 2.0 s | — | Meeting Hall |
| Sauna | 0.25 s | Bright | Sauna |
| MusicHall | 3.2 s | Lush | Music bus only |

- **Snow hush:** during snowfall, the Valley reverb send drops by up to −6 dB and its return is low-passed to 3 kHz. Distant sources get extra air absorption. Snow audibly muffles the world.
- **Master:** a DynamicsCompressor as gentle glue (ratio 2, knee 12, −18 dB threshold), then a brickwall-style limiter (a Compressor at ratio 20, 1 ms attack, −1.5 dBFS ceiling).
- **Scheduler:** a lookahead clock (25 ms tick, 120 ms horizon) schedules music notes and timed SFX on `audioContext.currentTime`. Ticks run on a Worker timer, so they keep time when the main thread is busy. Tabs playing audio aren't timer-throttled by browsers, so music continues in the background.

### 6.3 The Sound Foundry (offline synthesis from code)
- **What it is:** a pure-TypeScript DSP library with no DOM (`src/dsp/`): oscillators, noise (white, pink, brown), envelopes, biquads, one-poles, comb/allpass filters, modal resonator banks, additive partial banks, FM operators, Karplus–Strong, granular scatter, waveshaper, simple convolution.
- **Recipes** are deterministic functions `(params, seed) → Float32Array`.
- **Where it runs:**
  - In a **Web Worker pool** (2–3 workers) at startup. Results transfer to the main thread as AudioBuffers.
  - In **Node** for tests, metrics and the Sound Board exports (the same code).
- **Render order:**
  1. **Essential set** (title theme instruments, UI, footsteps, core chopping): < 1.5 s on a 4-core machine. Rendered during the boot splash.
  2. **Core gameplay set:** < 6 s, in the background during the title screen.
  3. **Area sets:** lazily, when approaching lake, farm or ridge content.
- **Memory budget:** ≤ 64 MB of PCM total.
  - Mono renders. 32 kHz for dark instruments (felt piano, pads), 44.1 kHz for bright effects (chimes, sparkle, sizzles).
  - Instruments are sampled every 4 semitones (±2 st pitch shift at playback) with 2 velocity layers. Tails are capped at 4–6 s with a natural fade.
- **Variants:** each SFX recipe renders N variants from seeds with jittered parameters: exciter noise colour, resonance frequencies ±4%, decay ±10%, micro-timing of layers.
  - Playback adds pitch ±3–6%, gain ±1.5 dB, and a random low-pass cutoff ±10%.
  - A no-repeat shuffle avoids playing the same variant twice in a row.

### 6.4 Music system
**Structure (`src/music/` is pure logic that emits note events; `src/view/audio/MusicPlayer.ts` plays them)**
- `Theory.ts`: scales and modes, chord construction, Roman-numeral parsing, voice leading (minimum total semitone motion among candidate voicings within register bounds).
- `Piece` (data): key, mode, meter, tempo, sections (Intro, A, B, Interlude, Outro) with chord progressions, **motif cells**, instrumentation per **mood variant** (clear, snow, storm, night), density and register ranges, and a form grammar.
- `Performers`: **Pad, Keys, Melody, Bass, Sparkle (bells), Texture, Perc** (only some pieces). Each turns the chord stream into timed note events using pattern libraries and the variation rules.
- `Director`: chooses what to play from game context, handles transitions, rests and stingers, and exposes `currentKey`, `currentChord` and `nextBeat` to pitched SFX.
- `Stingers`: short in-key flourishes quantized to the next beat.
- `Radio`: separate diegetic stations with their own generative songs.

**Variation engine (how the music never loops)**
- **Form:** each performance picks a form from a grammar, e.g. `Intro(2) → A(8) → A'(8) → B(8) → [Interlude(4)] → A''(8) → Outro(2–4)`, with probabilistic omissions and repeats. That gives a 2–4 minute performance.
- **Harmony:** each chord has weighted substitutions (I → Imaj7, I6/9, I/3; IV → IVadd9, ii7; V → Vsus4→V, V7, iii). Inversions keep bass lines smooth.
- **Voicing:** chosen from close, open or spread by minimum voice movement. Low-register mud is avoided (no thirds below C3).
- **Keys patterns:** a library per meter.
  - 3/4: 1-5-10, 1-5-8-10-8-5, rolled block.
  - 4/4: 1-5-8-10 rolling, broken 1-5-3-5, sustained pedal plus top voice.
  - 6/8: 1-5-8-10-8-5.
  - A density parameter drops notes probabilistically.
- **Melody:** motif cells (rhythm + contour in scale degrees) with transformations: fit-to-chord, inversion, retrograde, augmentation ×2, fragmentation, sequence ±step, and ornaments (grace notes, appoggiaturas).
  - Phrases are 2 bars with breathing rests. Melody sounds in 50–70% of bars.
  - **Call and response** between instruments (piano phrase, bells answer).
- **Humanize:** timing ±12 ms, velocity ±8%, slow tempo drift ±3%, release-length variance.
- **Dynamics:** section swells (crescendo into B, diminuendo into the Outro).
- **Leitmotif:** a fragment of the **Wrenhollow Lullaby** appears in about 20% of phrases across all pieces. Discovered **constellation motifs** join the night pieces' vocabulary. **Character motifs** play on bells when an arc scene starts.
- **Anti-repetition:**
  - The Director keeps 60 minutes of history.
  - The same piece doesn't repeat in a zone within 12 minutes if alternatives exist. If not, the mood variant, form and seed must differ.
  - An automated soak test asserts that no identical 4-bar event sequence recurs within 10 minutes (Part 7.10).

**Silence and density**
- **Rests after each performance:**

| Music frequency setting | Rest length |
|---|---|
| Often | 20–60 s |
| Sometimes (default) | 45–150 s |
| Rarely | 120–300 s |
| Off | Only ambience, stingers and diegetic music |

- During rests, ambience and diegetic sound carry the scene.
- **Work-aware density:** loud work in the last 10 s (chop, saw, split, plow) gives melody density −30%, keys −20% and music −2 dB, so the tactile sounds lead.
- **Idle-aware:** sitting, stargazing or time-lapse raises density a little and favours melody.
- **Blizzard:** the score becomes *more* continuous and warmer, with longer pads and shorter rests. Comfort beats drama.

**Transitions**
- **Zone change:** wait for the next bar line (at most 1 bar), then crossfade 6–8 s. The incoming piece starts with its pad Intro, and a shared common-tone pad bridges the two keys.
- **Entering or leaving buildings:** no piece change. Apply the "interior" music EQ (−2 dB above 6 kHz, +1 dB at 200 Hz). Exception: **arriving home** lets the current phrase resolve, then Hearthside takes over.
- **Weather:** switch mood variant at the next section boundary.
- **Time of day:** the next performance draws from the new pool; the current one finishes naturally.
- **Truck radio on:** the score fades out over 3 s as the radio fades in. **Radio off:** the score continues with a subtle cab EQ.
- **Festivals:** festival pieces override the zone pool for their duration.
- **Dialogue:** −3 dB duck. Arc scenes may request a piece or stinger.

### 6.5 Instrument recipes (Sound Foundry)

| Instrument | Method | Key parameters |
|---|---|---|
| **Felt piano** | Additive modal: 16–24 partials, inharmonicity `f_n = n·f0·√(1+B·n²)` (B 0.0002–0.0008 by register), per-partial decays `τ_n = τ₁/(1 + 0.35n)`, two-stage decay (prompt + aftersound), **felt hammer** = 8 ms low-passed noise (1.2–3 kHz by velocity), soundboard body (3 peaking EQs at 110/400/2.2k), damper-release noise | Sampled every 4 semitones, A1–C7; 2 velocity layers; ≤ 5 s tails; 32 kHz |
| Celesta / music box | Modal bars (1, 3.93, 9.1) / tines (1, 5.4, 8.9, 15) with fast high decay, metallic attack click | 44.1 kHz |
| Bells / glass | 2-op FM (ratio 1:3.5; index 3 → 0.5 decay), long 6–8 s decay; "glass" uses ratio 1:1.41 | — |
| Marimba / woodblock | Modal (1, 4.0, 9.9), strong fundamental, mallet noise 3 ms | Ties to the "wood" theme |
| Plucked strings (guitar, harp, upright bass) | Karplus–Strong with loop low-pass, pluck-position comb, body EQ; strum = staggered onsets 12–25 ms | — |
| Flute / whistle | Sine + 2nd harmonic (−18 dB) + band-passed breath noise; delayed vibrato 5 Hz ±15 cents | Real-time voices |
| Reed (clarinet, accordion) | Pulse/square (odd harmonics), LP 2–4 kHz; accordion = musette detune pair ±6 cents | Real-time |
| Bowed cello-ish | Saw → body formants (250/800/1.5k) + slow attack + vibrato + bow noise | Real-time |
| **Warm pad** | 3 detuned saws per voice (±7 cents) → LP with slow cutoff LFO (0.05 Hz) → chorus (2 modulated delays); attack 1.5–3 s | Real-time, ≤ 16 oscillators |
| Choir ("ooh/aah") | Saw/pulse → 3 formant band-passes (ooh 300/870/2240, aah 730/1090/2440) + vibrato + 3-voice ensemble detune | Real-time, Lantern Night |
| Fiddle | Saw + bow noise + formant body + expressive vibrato | Real-time, festivals and radio |
| Brushes / shaker / bodhrán | Filtered-noise envelopes; bodhrán = sine drop 120→60 Hz + noise click | Pre-rendered variants |

**Real-time voice budget:** at most 24 simultaneous music voices. Pre-rendered voices are cheap: an AudioBufferSourceNode plus a gain each.

### 6.8 Footstep and surface system
- **Resolution order** at each footfall event:
  1. Interior floor material (planks, stone, rug).
  2. Snow patch cell: depth < 2 cm → the underlying surface (flagstone, gravel, planks, asphalt, ice); otherwise fresh snow.
  3. Trail map: packed ≥ 120 → packed snow; otherwise fresh snow (deep if depth > 25 cm).
  4. Lake or creek ice.
  5. Rock.
- **Layers per step:**
  - Surface texture.
  - Boot heel layer by boot tier.
  - Crampon/snowshoe layer if equipped.
  - Clothing rustle (every step, −12 dB).
  - Jog steps are louder and brighter (+2 dB, +600 Hz tilt).
- **Cold squeak:** packed snow at ≤ −15°C adds the squeak layer (a real phenomenon, delightful to notice).
- **Variation:** no immediate variant repeat, pitch ±5%, gain ±1.5 dB, L/R foot pan ±0.05.
- NPC footsteps use the same system with their own boot type, attenuated.

### 6.9 Spatial audio
- **Listener:** position at the player's head, orientation from the camera. Panning matches the screen, and distance is relative to the character.
- **PannerNode:** HRTF on High and Ultra, equal-power otherwise (setting). Distance model inverse: ref 2 m, rolloff 1.2, max 80 m (festival bonfire 120 m).
- **Air absorption:** a per-emitter low-pass at `min(18k, 20k × e^(−d/40)) Hz`, floored at 1.5 kHz. Distance is scaled ×1.4 during snowfall (snow absorbs).
- **Occlusion by zones** (no raycasts):

| Situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Emitter and listener in different interior/exterior zones | LP 800 Hz, −9 dB |
| Through an open door | LP 2.5 kHz, −4 dB |
| Truck cab (windows closed) | Exterior LP 1.2 kHz, −6 dB |

- **Voice management:** at most 32 positional one-shots and 12 positional loops. Priority stealing (lowest priority, quietest, oldest). Emitters are pooled, and any node graph not playing is disconnected.
- **Doppler:** faked for the truck and train via small playbackRate shifts from radial velocity (±3%).

### 6.11 Audio QA (how an AI developer verifies sound it cannot hear)
1. **Offline render harness** (`tools/audio-render.ts`, Node, the same Foundry code): renders every SFX variant and 3-minute music performances per context. It writes WAVs, **spectrogram PNGs** and a JSON report.
   - Checks: peak ≤ −1 dBFS pre-master, no DC offset (< 0.001), **no clicks** (sample-delta outliers at starts, ends and loop seams), attack/decay within recipe bounds, spectral centroid within family range, loudness within ±2 LU of family target.
2. **In-browser integration test** (Playwright + OfflineAudioContext): renders a scripted 60 s gameplay mix (footsteps, chop, split, stack, music) through the real graph. It checks bus levels, ducking behaviour and the limiter never exceeding the ceiling.
3. **Repetition statistics:** a 60-minute simulated Director run (sped up, event-level, not audio) asserts no identical 4-bar event sequence within 10 minutes, rests present, and piece variety per zone.
4. **The Sound Board** (`/dev/sound-board`, deployed with playtest builds): every sound and variant, every music piece in every mood, and every ambience scene, with play buttons, loudness readouts and a **notes field that exports feedback JSON**. The user listens and comments; the developer applies the notes. **This is the main quality gate for "excellent" audio.**
