## Part 1 — Game Summary and Core Loop

### 1.1 One-page summary
**Hearthwood** is a slow, warm, low-poly third-person game set through one long winter in **Wrenhollow**, a mountain town of about two hundred people beside frozen **Stillmere Lake** and below **Graybeard Ridge**. Old Hal Brennan's back has finally given out. He hands you his cabin at the forest edge, his woodlot, his tools and his faded red pickup *Marigold* (flat battery). The town needs someone to keep its stoves fed.

You fell trees and saw them into rounds. You split the rounds at the chopping block, which is timing-based but has no fail state, and stack the pieces in your woodshed, where they season over a few days. Then you load *Marigold* and drive through the falling snow to deliver: to Mara's café oven, Margo's lakeside sauna, the Lindqvist farm's sugar shack, the old inn with its fifteen fireplaces. Snow keeps falling, so you shovel paths (yours and your neighbours', for a few coins and a thank-you), rake roofs and, once you own the blade, plow roads. That opens the lake road and the farm lane. Chains later get you up the icy switchbacks to the ridge's old oaks and the fire lookout, where you can watch the aurora.

Cold is gentle. A warmth meter drains outdoors, faster at night and in wind. Low warmth makes you slow and shivery and suggests going home. It never hurts you. Fires, hot cocoa, the sauna and better wool fix it.

Wrenhollow is a living town of 14 named people with routines, opinions, friendships and small stories. They move forward as you deliver, chat and help. Examples: Hal carving birds for everyone, Freya choosing between the stars and the farm, Margo's legendary pike. Seven invented festivals mark the winter, from the **Hearth Fair** stacking contest to **Lantern Night**, where the whole town gathers around a bonfire built from your wood.

Everything is designed to feel good to do and to be around. Upgrades change the feel as well as the numbers: a heavier *thock* from the maul, chains jingling on the truck, the wool coat's softer rustle, a bigger woodpile. The music is generated on the fly and is always slightly different. It follows the weather and the hour, then falls silent for a while so the fire and the wind can be heard. You can play for hours or leave it running and listen to the snow.

**Pillars (every decision is checked against these):**
1. **Tactile satisfaction.** Every action has anticipation, impact, follow-through, particles, layered sound and a visible world change.
2. **Calm.** Soft palette, gentle audio, no fail states, no punishing timers, no nagging.
3. **A living town.** Routines, relationships, gossip that reacts to what you do, and visible consequences such as porch woodpiles growing and windows lighting up.
4. **Gentle progression.** Steady, meaningful upgrades that open places and new ways to play. Never a grind.

**Pillar tests (used in reviews):** Does it feel good with the sound off? With the screen off? Could a stressed player relax doing this? Does the town notice? Does the upgrade change feel, not just a number?

### 1.2 The core loop at three zoom levels
- **Moment (5–30 s):** swing, chop, saw, split, stack, shovel. Each action is a small rhythm with a juicy payoff (Part 2.5 and the juice table in Part 9).
- **Session (10–40 min):** plan a little ("It's a cold snap Thursday, oak sells high, and I've got 12 bundles seasoning"). Produce wood, do a delivery run, shovel a job, chat, warm up, buy something.
- **Winter (days and weeks):** friendships deepen, arcs unfold, new areas open, festivals arrive, the woodshed grows, the town changes.

### 1.3 The first 10 minutes
- **0:00.** Title screen: the cabin at dusk with snow falling and the felt-piano main theme ("Wrenhollow Lullaby"). *Continue/New Game*. The camera drifts down through the snow into the game with no loading cut.
- **0:30.** Late afternoon, first real snowfall of the season. Hal's pickup coughs and dies at the bottom of the cabin drive. Hal (tall, red plaid, flat cap, bad back) laughs: "She always does that when she's cold. Just like me." He walks you up the path. Your boots crunch and you leave the first footprints in fresh snow.
- **1:30.** The woodshed is half full of Hal's old seasoned stack: eight bundles (5 pine, 3 birch). Beside the chopping block are five small rounds (about 22 cm) that Hal never split. Small rounds are sized so a well-timed strike with the dull Old Axe still splits cleanly (Part 2.5.6). Prompt: *[Hold LMB] Raise axe*. You lift the axe, and at the top it catches a small glint of light and a soft chime. You release. *THOCK.* Brief hit-stop, a light camera kick, bark chips, and the round cleaves into two halves that tumble into the snow. A two-note chime plays in the music's current key. Hal: "Ha! You've done this before. Or you're lucky. Both are fine."
- **3:00.** You split the rest. Pieces heap beside the block. *[E] Gather armload*: ten pieces stack neatly in your arms. You walk to the shed and *[E] Stack*. Each piece clacks into place, the notes rising like a small scale, and the stack visibly grows.
- **5:00.** Hal points at a lean pine at the woodlot edge. A dotted fall-line appears on the snow. Eight swings with the old axe, each with creaks and snow shaking off the branches. On the last one the tree cracks, tilts slowly, whooshes down and lands with a *WHUMP*, a cloud of powder and a gentle camera shake. Two crows leave the neighbouring pine. The world goes quiet and the music leaves a rest.
- **6:30.** You saw one round off the trunk with the rusty bucksaw by alternating strokes (hold-to-auto-saw is available). Hal: "That saw's older than your parents. Ines can put a new blade on it."
- **8:30.** The clock is frozen during Hal's intro and starts at 15:00 when the tree lands. By now it's dusk and the windows glow amber. Warmth dips slightly and your breath gets bigger. Hal lights the cabin stove and hands you his old lantern: "Nights are long up here. Keep this. And keep the fire fed. It keeps you, too."
- **9:00.** Hal leaves a note on the table: *"Mara at the café needs two bundles of birch for her oven. Truck's dead, so it's your arms and your legs for now. —H."* The first order appears in the Notebook. You step onto the porch and see the town lights twinkling below. The bell in the town hall rings six times across the valley.

### 1.4 The first hour
Timing check: the clock runs 90 s per game hour by day and 45 s at night (Part 2.1). The first real hour is about Day 1 evening plus most of Day 2.
- **10–17 min (Day 1, 17:00–21:00).** Walk down the snowy Cabin Road into Wrenhollow carrying one bundle, the hand capacity. The music changes from "Woodlot" to "Main Street" as the town lights come into view. Deliver birch to Mara at **The Kettle** café. She pays 7c and gives you a free cocoa: warmth +20, "Toasty" for an hour. Her son Pip asks if you're the new "wood wizard." The **First Snow Supper** is on at the Meeting Hall. Dropping in is optional, and every townsperson greets you once there.
- **17–20 min.** Walk home under the stars with your lantern. Feed the stove and sleep. The Day 1 ledger card: "Delivered 1 bundle. Met 9 neighbours."
- **20–35 min (Day 2 morning).** There's fresh snow overnight. At the post office notice board, Bea's cards list three shoveling jobs: Gus's store steps, the school path and the inn walkway, 4–7c each. Shoveling feels great. The scrape rings on the flagstones, chunks arc onto a growing bank, and the path is visibly clean. Gus grunts a thank-you and sells you a canvas **log tote** (12c, carries 2 bundles).
- **35–50 min.** Deliver the second birch bundle and meet Rusty at the garage. *Marigold* needs a battery (40c). You have enough: Hal's 20c "starter", the 10c he paid for splitting his rounds, the café, and the jobs. Rusty gives you a ride up in his wrecker, fits the battery, and *Marigold* coughs, catches and rumbles. The radio crackles into "Radio Wrenhollow", a jaunty old-time waltz. This is the first big moment.
- **50–60 min.** You drive six of Hal's seasoned bundles down to Gus's store to sell wholesale. On the way you notice that deep snow slows you on foot, while your own trodden path has become packed and quicker. Dusk falls and the hall bell rings. You drive home through blue snow in the amber glow of the headlights and read the **Ledger**: "Day 2: Earned 39c."

### 1.5 Hour ten
It is Day 21, a week before Lantern Night. You own a splitting maul, a Swedish bow saw, a wheelbarrow and a sled. The woodshed has two bays and you're saving for ventilated walls. *Marigold* has snow tires, a bigger bed and a plow blade that reopened the Lake Road. Your wool mackinaw and trapper hat keep you out past dark.

A usual morning starts with the radio forecast: "a real cold snap Thursday, folks." Oak and birch demand is up. You plow your drive and the farm lane, a paid job for Otto, then load 18 bundles for three stops. The sauna gets pine for Margo. The Snowdrift Inn gets birch, and Wendell pays extra because guests love white bark by the hearth. The Lindqvist farm gets oak, and Freya asks whether you've ever seen the aurora from the ridge. You'll need chains for that road; Rusty says 300c. Afternoon: chop in the woodlot while snow falls, split twenty rounds in a rhythm, and hear the stacking scale ring out as the second bay fills. At dusk you drive to the lake, drill a hole, and jig for perch next to Margo while she tells a tall tale about "Old Whiskers." Back in town the café is full. Hal and Gus are at checkers again, Bea is talking about the Lantern Night bonfire, and it's your wood they're counting on.

### 1.6 Hour twenty and beyond (late game, "just existing")
Nearly everything is unlocked. There's a porch swing, soon a masonry heater, a telescope from Freya on the porch and a wind chime tuned to the music. The Inn's standing contract and a mail-order crate on the Thursday train keep coins coming if you want them. You might spend an evening sitting on the swing with time sped up to watch snow bury the yard, or drive the long way round the lake with the radio on. At the lookout you trace the last constellation, "The Wren", as the aurora rolls overhead.
The town is fully alive and reacting: porch woodpiles stocked, a bird book half full, a snowman the kids keep adding to. Year 2 brings back the festivals with new small moments.
